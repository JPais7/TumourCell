# Scientific novelty gate

The color is a decision category, not a probability.

| Direction | Rating | Evidence-based judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Discover TNBC clusters associated with chemotherapy response | RED | Yan 2026 already analyzes 101 patients and 44 spatial cases. |
| Build a pretreatment ecotype response classifier | RED/ORANGE | Yan 2026 reports ecotypes and a 13-gene panel; another classifier is incremental without superior external validation. |
| Map immune changes during checkpoint therapy | RED | Bassez, Zhang, Shiao, Wang, and Hammerl cover this extensively. |
| Reproduce malignant functional programs across breast subtypes | ORANGE | Useful validation work, but Wu, Pal, Yan and pan-cancer program studies establish recurrence. |
| Cross-study, clone-aware selection-versus-plasticity decomposition | GREEN | Biologically important and incompletely externally tested; identifiability is the central risk. |
| Depth-calibrated test of whether residual programs were truly absent pretreatment | GREEN | Methodologically specific and falsifiable; novelty requires final systematic confirmation. |
| Spatial niches constrain future residual-state entry | YELLOW/GREEN | Important but current public spatial series may not supply matched temporal tissue. |
| Patient-specific continuous-time cell-state digital twin | YELLOW conceptually; RED feasibility | Novel claims would outrun data; sparse destructive biopsies do not identify patient-specific dynamics. |

## Closest competitors

Kim 2018 is the closest biological result. Yan 2026 is the closest high-powered state/response framework. GSE299631 is the closest emerging residual-disease spatial framework. TumorTwin and MRI-based breast twins are the closest engineering concepts, but operate at different data/scales.

## Gate outcome

Conditional pass only for the narrow selection-versus-plasticity question. A formal database search with documented strings, dates, screening criteria, and citation chasing is required before manuscript novelty claims.
